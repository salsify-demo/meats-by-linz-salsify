Foodservice, retail, direct. One product story.

Meats by Linz supplies dry-aged and portion-controlled beef to steakhouses nationwide, sells Linz Heritage Angus direct to consumers online, and has moved into grocery retail. Why it matters now

Retail and direct-to-consumer run on structured product content

Foodservice sells on relationships and samples. Retail and online sell on the product page: weight, cut, aging, grade, origin, nutrition and the story behind Linz Heritage Angus. Each grocery retailer and marketplace wants that data in its own template, and consumer regulations add labeling fields on top. Salsify is how a foodservice brand builds that content once and publishes it everywhere.

Grocery retail

Retailers and online grocers each have a product content spec: attributes, images, nutrition panels, claims. Rejections stall the launch.

Direct to consumer

The Linz site needs rich pages that explain aging, cuts and the Heritage Angus program, and keep them consistent with retail.

Compliance and claims

USDA grade, aging claims, allergen and nutrition data have to be correct on every listing.

The pattern we see

Where product content usually breaks down

We have not audited Linz's systems. What follows is the pattern we see at foodservice brands expanding into retail and direct-to-consumer and the questions we would like to check against yours.

Content built for foodservice

  • Product details live in sales sheets, the ERP and the heads of the sales team
  • Consumer-facing copy, nutrition and claims are written per channel as needed
  • Images and the brand story are recreated for each new partner

Retail templates by hand

  • Each grocer and marketplace wants a different attribute set and image spec
  • Nutrition, grade and aging claims are re-entered per listing
  • Errors surface at the retailer, after the fact, delaying the launch

3. ValidateReadiness reports check the record against every destination's current spec and image rules.
